The Stone Algorithm: From Mass to Maintenance

$$ \text{LS:}\ (\text{Stone}, \text{Mass}) \;\longrightarrow\; \text{UB:}\ \text{Power}(t \mid \text{Body}) + \text{Drift} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{SGD:}\ \frac{d(\text{Work})}{dt} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{UI/UX:}\ \text{FP}^2 \pm \text{Risk} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{EC}\ \int \text{Survival}\,dt + \text{Ju stice} $$

1. LS: Latent Space → The Stone (The Prior)

The Stone (14 lbs) is the initial latent embedding. It is the a priori constant that reconciles the two fundamental domains of human existence: Biology (the neonate) and Trade (the merchandise). It represents the "human-carryable magnitude"—the fundamental unit of what matters ($x$) and what weighs ($E$).

2. UB: User Behavior $E(t\mid x)+\epsilon$ → The Walk (The Function)

The static stone becomes dynamic. This is the 86-year-old male walking for 45 minutes or the neonate on the hanging scale. The equation shifts from measuring mass to measuring capacity. The $\epsilon$ represents the biological noise—the stumble, the pause, the pulse drift.

3. SGD: $\frac{dE_x}{dt}$ → Power (The Diagnostic)

This is the hinge. We move from Weight (lbs) to Power (Watts).
$$77\,\text{W} \quad \text{vs} \quad 123\,\text{W}$$ The derivative of energy over time is the true measure of frailty. It is the slope of the gradient: how steep is the cost of living?

4. UI/UX: $\frac{dE_{\bar{x}}}{dt}\ \pm\ z\sqrt{\dots}$ → FP² (The Signal)

Here lies the upgrade to Linda Fried’s Phenotype (FP²). It is no longer a binary checklist (Frail/Not Frail). It is a continuous signal with a confidence interval. The term $\pm z\sqrt{\frac{d^2 E_x}{dt^2}}$ captures the volatility (fall risk, heart rate variability). It answers: "Is this system stable, or is it about to crash?"

5. EC: $\int E_x\,dt + \dots$ → The Integral (Survival & Justice)

The final integration. For the Neonate: The integral is Growth/Survival. For the Merchant: The integral is Value/Fairness. For the Digital Twin: The integral is the Life-Span/Health-Span.

The scale was always meant to measure the cost of remaining alive.
